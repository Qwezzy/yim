From the director

Growth, resilience and transformation.

The 2024–2025 financial year has been a period of growth, resilience, and transformation for YIM. Despite ongoing social and economic challenges, we remained committed to integrated, community-driven programmes that strengthen education, health, psychosocial wellbeing and livelihoods.

Director Hlobi Ngidi wrote in the 2023 report that the work is possible because of staff, volunteers, schools and donors who stay with communities after the campaign day ends. That same model now runs in Ekurhuleni, Mkhondo, Amsterdam, Rorke’s Drift and KwaThandeka.

Four streams of work

From the 2024–2025 annual report: education, health, psychosocial support and food security.

Education

After-school support, literacy and numeracy, 30 reading clubs, ECD support and 82 teaching assistants across 26 sites. YIM Education began in 2017. KZN has reached 4,240 learners across 11 schools in recent years.

Health

HIV testing, TB and STI screening, ART linkage, PrEP referrals, index testing and condom distribution — with WITS RHI, Isibani, Write to Care and the Department of Health.

Social & food

Home visits, support groups, parcels, and IDC gardens that trained women and youth, sold produce locally, and installed a solar borehole in KwaZulu-Natal.

Where the work happens

Urban, township, peri-urban and rural sites across three provinces.

Gauteng

Kingsway · Daveyton · Ekurhuleni · Benoni

Head office and the first communities YIM was formed to serve in 2004: Kingsway, Lindelani and Barcelona.

Mpumalanga

Mkhondo · Amsterdam

Rural after-school, SEF livelihoods, gardening, home-tracing and school-cleaning work since 2022.

KwaZulu-Natal

Rorke’s Drift · KwaThandeka · Nqutu

After-school teams at 11 schools, ECD support and the solar-powered borehole for food gardens.
